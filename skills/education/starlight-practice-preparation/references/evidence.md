# Evidence and practice boundaries

Reviewed 2026-09-09. Recheck source-specific guidance before an instructor release.

| Topic | Defensible explanation | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide and pH | Breathing faster/deeper than metabolic demand can lower CO2 and cause respiratory alkalosis, lightheadedness and tingling. | Tingling demonstrates healing, spiritual attainment or superior oxygen delivery. |
| Oxygen and retention | Breath holding can lower oxygen. Prior overbreathing can delay the urge to breathe; elapsed time is not a safety indicator. | A personal-best retention time measures health or spiritual progress. |
| Adrenaline/epinephrine | Kox et al. studied a small group of healthy volunteers trained in a combined meditation, cold and breathing intervention during experimental endotoxemia; epinephrine and immune-response changes were observed. | A SOMA-specific effect, a treatment for autoimmune disease, or the same response in an individual session. |
| Mood and arousal | Balban et al. studied brief daily structured breathing over a month; findings support potential mood/arousal benefits in the studied setting. | A cure, a guaranteed effect, or validation of every breathing method. |
| Dopamine, serotonin, oxytocin, endorphins, DMT | These are sometimes invoked in breathwork narratives. This pack has not established a SOMA-specific causal release profile for them. | A predictable chemical cocktail, percentages, pineal DMT activation, or lab results inferred from feelings. |
| Humming/nitric oxide | A separate physiology topic requiring a directly checked primary study before a quantitative claim. | Increased nasal NO proves systemic healing or clears an infection. |
| Imagery | Treat imagery as voluntary imagination, attention and rehearsal. | Literal entities, recovered memories, biological repair or future events established by vividness. |

## Sources and scope

- [Johns Hopkins: Hyperventilation](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/conditions-and-diseases/hyperventilation): clinical explanation of low CO2 and associated symptoms.
- [Kox et al., PNAS 2014](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1322174111): combined intervention; not a SOMA efficacy trial. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1322174111.
- [Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/): structured breathing RCT. DOI 10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895.
- [SOMA official FAQ](https://www.somabreath.com/faqs/): primary authority for what SOMA says about preparation and contraindications, not independent confirmation of its biomedical claims. Its retention cautions include pregnancy, epilepsy, pacemaker/arrhythmia and severe uncontrolled hypertension. Do not turn this short list into medical clearance.
- [SOMA app](https://www.somabreath.com/soma-app/): official practice delivery and retention cautions. Use the participant's authorized class or app for exact technique.

## How to improve without escalating

Improve consistency, comfortable posture, ability to follow an authorized facilitator's rhythm without strain, recovery and useful integration. Ask about one difficulty at a time. A missed session is information, not failure. Do not prescribe lower respiratory-rate targets, increasing retention schedules, hypoxic training, hyperventilation, bandhas, pelvic-floor clenching or pressure to the head. Refer technique corrections to the qualified instructor who can observe the person.

If someone requests baseline tracking, label the exact method and conditions as self-report; never translate seconds into diagnoses, VO2 max or progress rankings. Do not ask for repeated maximal tests. Improvements in daily functioning and repeatable comfortable practice are the relevant outcomes.
