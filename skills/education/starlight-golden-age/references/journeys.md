# Original Starlight journeys

These are new Starlight scripts. They do not reproduce SOMA Breath, a living teacher's lesson or the previously mentioned Golden Age Collection audio. Read at the participant's pace; silence and duration are flexible. Use ordinary breathing throughout. Stop immersion if the person is uncomfortable or disoriented.

## A small window — brief practice

Feel the place supporting you. Let your breathing take care of itself. You can keep your eyes open.

Imagine—or simply consider—a morning in a world you would be glad to help create. Begin close to home. There is enough quiet to hear another person finish a sentence. Something has been made with care: a room, a meal, a piece of music, a garden.

Notice one sign that people are treating each other well. Nothing needs to be perfect. Let the scene have ordinary work in it. Someone repairs a railing. Someone teaches a child. Someone makes a difficult conversation a little kinder.

Find your place in this morning. What are your hands doing? What quality are you bringing? Choose one detail you would like to carry with you.

Now notice the room you are actually in. Feel the support beneath you. Look around when comfortable. Move your fingers. You do not have to hold onto a special feeling.

Complete this sentence, aloud or silently: “Today, I can make a small part of that real by…” One action is enough. You may also simply rest.

## The city worth returning to — extended practice

### Arrive
Notice the contact between your body and its support. The room continues around you. There is nothing you need to achieve with your breath. Keep whatever degree of attention to the room feels comfortable.

For this exercise, imagine a possible future. It is a creative space you can change or leave whenever you choose. Words or a felt sense are enough when pictures do not come.

### Enter the morning
A path runs beside clear water. Early light touches the edges of leaves. Beyond the trees stands a city whose beauty comes from care: shaded streets, places to sit, homes where people can rest, workshops where useful things are made.

Choose one small detail. Perhaps a cup warming someone's hands. Perhaps the quiet click of a bicycle wheel. Stay with what is easy to imagine.

### See the people
Notice an ordinary exchange. Someone asks for help and receives a clear answer. Someone disagrees without humiliation. A young person is being taught a skill. An older person has time to tell a story.

This future has maintenance, mistakes and disagreement. Look for what helps people respond well. What is protected here? What has become easier for someone who used to be overlooked?

### Find your work
Enter a place where you could contribute. It might be a studio, a classroom, a kitchen, a garden or an open table. A task is waiting. It has a size you can understand. Other people have their own choices and responsibilities.

Notice the quality with which you begin. Steady attention. Patience. Courage. Play. Choose the word that fits, or none at all.

### Optional imagined guide
Use this passage only when the participant welcomes it. Otherwise remain with the place and the task.

You may imagine a figure representing a quality you value. You decide their appearance and distance. They have no authority over you. Think of them as another angle on your own question.

Ask: “What am I overlooking in the way I want to help?” Let an answer arise, or let there be silence. You are free to disagree with anything you imagine. Take only what remains useful when considered in ordinary life.

### Carry one thing
Choose one detail from this possible future: an act of care, a design choice, a way of listening, a piece of work. Let the rest of the scene fade. A whole world does not need to become today's assignment.

### Return
Notice the support beneath you again. Notice the sounds of your actual surroundings. Look around at a pace that feels comfortable. Move your hands and feet. Let the imagined scene become a memory of an exercise.

There is no need to interpret it immediately. When you feel ready, name one detail and what it means to you. Later, choose one action you control. The value of this exercise can be as ordinary as a conversation handled more carefully or a small useful thing completed.

## Instructor use
Read the participant's preferred version. Leave optional pauses; do not synchronize the script to retention. Keep an eyes-open route. After the return, offer a private paper note as an equal alternative to AI capture. Invite one observation; do not pressure sharing or interpret a participant's experience for them.
